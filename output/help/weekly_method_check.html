Target and treatment coding Section 2.1, pp. 3-4, Eq. (1) fit_lrd(), fit_alrd(), predict_ite(): the design uses signed T times the basis divided by 2, and predictions return the full treated-minus-control contrast. Numeric 0/1 and -1/1 give the same fitted coefficients in the new tests. Baseline treatment constancy is assumed but not yet enforced.
Direct and augmented losses pp. 5-6, Eqs. (3), (5), (6) The unpenalized LRD fit and both ALRD stages match independent matrix GLS calculations for fixed covariance and positive subject-constant weights. Omitting the common 1/n factor does not change these unpenalized minimizers.
Main-effect versus ITE basis Section 2.3, p. 6 The paper permits separate classes G and F. The package uses one basis for both: a supported restriction, not the full framework.
Propensity weighting Section 2.1, p. 4; Section 2.5, p. 9 The code correctly uses p for treated subjects and 1-p for controls. ps = NULL assumes 0.5 in LRD but estimates scores from long-data outcome covariates in ALRD. Use explicit design probabilities, or baseline subject-level scores mapped to visits. Arbitrary visit-varying weights are not covered by the paper’s scalar subject-weighted loss.
Working covariance Section 2.2, p. 5; Section 2.5, p. 9 Actual numeric time gaps agree with the manuscript. Cholesky whitening reproduces fixed-covariance GLS. Unit-diagonal working correlation is narrower than a full variance model. The package’s residual-based rho updates differ from the EM-type approach discussed in the paper.

What I updated

I selected one bounded implementation task: covariance validity. The package now rejects non-finite/non-numeric times, exchangeable rho at or below −1/(m−1)-1/(m-1), duplicate AR(1) times, and negative AR(1) rho with fractional gaps. AR(1) matrices are checked for numerical positive definiteness. Estimated exchangeable rho respects the largest subject’s lower bound, and fractional-gap AR(1) estimation searches a nonnegative interval. Help pages and the user guide were updated accordingly.

The regression tests also check valid positive and negative correlations, unequal visit counts, both numeric treatment codings, inverse received-arm probabilities, and the LRD/main-effect/ITE matrix objectives. This checks the implementation; it is not a new simulation study or proof of consistency.

What remains unresolved

Theorem 2.1 (pp. 6-7) states population identification under the causal assumptions and a correct propensity or limiting main-effect function. It does not by itself validate finite-sample lasso, restricted/misspecified ITE bases, or the full CV pipeline. In particular, estimating the main effect by Eq. (5) with incorrect propensity weights need not recover m merely because the chosen basis contains m. This condition needs methodological clarification before a blanket double- robustness claim. No change to that estimator was made here.

For example, with constant covariates, true p=0.8p = 0.8, supplied p=0.5p = 0.5, m=0m = 0 and δ=2\delta = 2, the two population least-squares stages give

m̂=0.6,δ̂=1.28. \widehat{m} = 0.6, \qquad \widehat{\delta} = 1.28.

This algebraic check concerns the nuisance estimator’s limit; it does not contradict the theorem’s premise that the limiting m is true.

The lasso uses glmnet’s transformed-data scaling/standardization, so numerical lambda values should not be equated directly to Eq. (8) without a scaling derivation. The quadratic spline penalty in Eq. (9) is not implemented. Prediction factor/NA handling, zero weights, and full-pipeline resampling remain on the existing backlog. They are not additional assignments for this week.
